Week in brief

Managed Home Screen gains two RBAC permissions in Intune’s February release

The clearest service-facing change is the planned addition of two Managed Home Screen permissions to Intune’s School Administrator and Help Desk Operator roles. Most other high-value updates are documentation-led: Intune added or reorganized Windows feature, quality, and driver update guidance, made support boundaries and prerequisites more explicit, and documented audit-log retention as up to two years rather than one.

  • Intune · Tenant administration
    Managed Home Screen adds two RBAC permissions

    The February Intune release adds TemporarilySuspendManagedHomeScreen and RestoreManagedHomeScreen to the School Administrator and Help Desk Operator built-in roles. Microsoft recommends reviewing role assignments and updating internal documentation; no action is required to enable the change.

  • Intune · Device updates
    Hotpatch guidance specifies Windows 11 and Arm64 preview requirements

    New Intune guidance describes deploying Hotpatch through Intune and Autopatch on Windows 11 Enterprise 24H2 or later. Arm64 support is in public preview and requires disabling CHPE with the HotPatchRestrictions=1 registry setting and restarting devices; the guidance also calls out VBS and the latest baseline release.

  • Intune · Device updates
    Feature-update policy guidance covers rollout and safeguard behavior

    A new page explains how to create and assign Windows feature update policies, configure rollout timing, manage optional or required updates, and account for safeguard holds and multi-policy behavior. It provides a documented deployment workflow rather than announcing a separate product launch.

  • Intune · Device updates
    Driver-update guidance defines licensing and environment limits

    Intune Driver updates policies are documented as public-cloud-only and requiring Intune Plan 1 plus a Windows license with the Autopatch entitlement. GCC High, DoD, and LTSC environments are listed as unsupported, so administrators should verify tenant and licensing eligibility before deployment.

  • Intune · Device security
    Intune audit-log retention is documented as up to two years

    The Privacy Data Store Process documentation changes the stated Intune audit-log retention period from one year to up to two years for security purposes. Administrators can incorporate the longer documented availability into security and compliance planning.

For Intune administrators

Review Managed Home Screen role assignments and internal RBAC documentation as part of February release planning; no administrator action is required. For Windows update deployments, validate the documented platform, licensing, diagnostic-data, and policy prerequisites—especially the Hotpatch Arm64 preview conditions and the public-cloud and Autopatch licensing limits for Driver updates. Security and compliance teams should account for the documented two-year audit-log availability.
